## Abstract

This paper considers the Minimum Time Search (MTS) problem using multiple Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), where the fleet must be assigned distinct flight trajectories to minimize the expected total time to detect the target. Based on a Bayesian search model, we propose the EMPSO (Evolutionary Multimodal Particle Swarm Optimization) algorithm, which is a multimodal PSO variant capable of generating multiple search trajectories in a single run. EMPSO encodes each solution as a motion sequence and establishes neighborhoods based on measured distances to form effective search niches. The algorithm's search mechanism combines a simple position update method for local exploitation within niches and Differential Evolution operators for global exploration across the entire population, followed by an elitist selection step to extract a set of trajectories for the fleet. The proposed method was evaluated across six scenarios and compared against other multimodal PSO variants as well as other meta-heuristic algorithms. The results demonstrate that EMPSO achieves the best or highly competitive search performance across these scenarios with reasonable computational cost. Field experiments with a team of simultaneously flying UAVs further confirm that the generated trajectories can be executed as feasible coordinated missions on actual hardware.

## Highlights

1. **One run, N paths.** Multi-UAV minimum-time search is cast as a multimodal optimization problem over motion-encoded paths, so each optimum is a feasible flight path and one run yields the whole fleet plan.
2. **Niching in path space.** Neighborhoods are built from distances between *decoded trajectories*, not between motion encodings, so particles that fly similar routes are grouped even when their encodings differ.
3. **Hybrid update.** Locally informed PSO guidance refines paths inside each niche, while differential evolution mutation keeps the population diverse; greedy selection keeps only improvements.
4. **Fleet-aware extraction.** An elitist procedure picks the N personal bests that jointly reduce the expected detection time the most, instead of the N individually best paths.
5. **Validated on hardware.** Best or highly competitive results on six benchmark scenarios, and a field test in which three UAVs execute the planned paths concurrently.

## Why Minimum Time Search?

Time decides the outcome of a search and rescue operation: in avalanche burials, for example, the probability of survival drops sharply within the first half hour. The planning objective is therefore not to maximize the probability that the target is *eventually* found, but to minimize the *expected time* until it is found.

This is hard for three reasons. The target location is uncertain and represented by a probability map. Sensors are imperfect, so a negative observation lowers but does not eliminate the probability of an observed cell, which makes the value of each visit depend on the path flown so far. And feasible paths must be connected sequences of admissible moves, so the number of candidates grows exponentially with the horizon; the optimal searcher path problem is NP-hard.

For a fleet, running a single-path optimizer once per UAV is not enough: independently planned paths ignore each other and tend to pile onto the same high-probability region. The optimizer has to find **N feasible and spatially distinct paths** that complement each other.

## Problem Formulation

The search area is partitioned into $G$ cells, and the belief map $b(x_0)$ holds the probability that the target is in each cell. Each of the $N$ UAVs carries a sensor that reports detection or non-detection; since the sensors are independent, the probability that no sensor detects the target at step $t$ is

$$
p(\bar D_t \mid x_t) = \prod_{s=1}^{N} p(\bar D^s_t \mid x_t).
$$

The belief evolves by recursive Bayesian estimation. A prediction step propagates it with the target's Markov motion model, and an update step fuses the new observations:

$$
\hat b(x_t) = \sum_{x_{t-1} \in G} p(x_t \mid x_{t-1})\, b(x_{t-1}),
\qquad
b(x_t) = \eta_t \prod_{s=1}^{N} p(z^s_t \mid x_t)\, \hat b(x_t).
$$

The probability of not detecting the target at step $t$, and throughout steps $1..t$, are

$$
r_t = \sum_{x_t \in G} p(\bar D_t \mid x_t)\, \hat b(x_t),
\qquad
R_t = \prod_{k=1}^{t} r_k .
$$

With constant UAV speed, the expected time to detection over a horizon of $T$ steps reduces (in time steps) to

$$
J^* = \sum_{t=1}^{T} R_t ,
$$

and the goal is to find flight paths for the $N$ UAVs that minimize $J^*$.

## Method

  <Picture slot="figure" src={figStructure} alt="Block diagram of the EMPSO algorithm" maxHeight="max-h-[80svh]" />
  <Fragment slot="caption"><strong>Structure of EMPSO.</strong> Shaded blocks mark the three new components (niching in path space, hybrid update, extraction of multiple paths); white blocks are inherited from motion-encoded PSO, locally informed PSO, and DE.</Fragment>

Each generation repeats three steps: **niching in path space** groups particles by the distance between their decoded paths; the **hybrid update** moves each particle toward its neighborhood guide or by a DE mutation, followed by crossover and greedy selection; **extraction** builds the fleet one path at a time, each time adding the path that reduces the fleet cost the most. The best fleet found over all generations is returned.

### 1. Motion-Encoded Paths

Following motion-encoded PSO, a particle is not a list of cells but a sequence of movement vectors $U_i = (u_{i,1}, \dots, u_{i,T})$ with $u_{i,t} = (x_{i,t}, y_{i,t})$. Only the heading of each vector is kept, rounded to the nearest multiple of $45^\circ$:

$$
\alpha^*_{i,t} = 45^\circ \left\lfloor \operatorname{atan2}(y_{i,t}, x_{i,t}) / 45^\circ \right\rceil .
$$

Starting from the common start cell, the path is decoded as $o_{i,t} = o_{i,t-1} + u^*_{i,t}$, where $u^*_{i,t}$ is one of the eight unit steps. Decoding always yields a connected sequence of adjacent cells, so **every particle is a flyable path**, while the search itself runs in the continuous *motion space*.

</div>
</CarouselSlide>

<CarouselSlide>
<div className="method-slide method-slide-green">

### 2. Niching in Path Space

Euclidean distance between encodings does not measure how far apart two flights are: headings that differ by $1^\circ$ can round to different moves, and very different headings can decode to the same path. EMPSO therefore measures distance between the **decoded** paths $O_a$ and $O_b$:

$$
D(U_a, U_b) = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \lVert o_{a,t} - o_{b,t} \rVert_2 .
$$

For each particle, the $m$ particles closest to its personal best form its neighborhood. Because neighborhoods reflect spatial separation, the niches that form correspond to distinct regions of the belief map.

### 3. Hybrid Update

Each particle picks one of two operators at random in every generation. **Neighborhood guidance** (exploitation) moves it toward $\hat U_{i,k}$, a random weighted average of its neighbors' personal bests; **DE mutation** (exploration) perturbs a random individual by a scaled difference of two others:

$$
U^m_{i,k} =
\begin{cases}
(1-\lambda)\, U_{i,k} + \lambda\, \hat U_{i,k}, & \text{if } \mathrm{rand} < 0.5,\\[2pt]
U_{n_1,k} + F \odot \left(U_{n_2,k} - U_{n_3,k}\right), & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

The candidate then goes through per-movement crossover with probability $p_{Cr}$, and **greedy selection** keeps it only if its decoded path has a lower cost $J^*$. Every particle's cost is therefore non-increasing, and a mutation is accepted only when it finds a better path.

</div>
</CarouselSlide>

<CarouselSlide>
<div className="method-slide method-slide-violet">

### 4. Extraction of Multiple Paths

The $N$ individually best particles are not the best fleet: two strong paths may cover the same cells. For a set of paths $\mathcal P$, the joint cost multiplies the non-detection likelihoods of all UAVs in the set:

$$
J^*(\mathcal P) = \sum_{t=1}^{T} R_t(\mathcal P),
\qquad
p(\bar D_t \mid x_t) = \prod_{h \in \mathcal P} p(\bar D^h_t \mid x_t).
$$

Starting from the empty fleet ($J^*(\varnothing) = T$), EMPSO greedily adds the personal best with the largest gain $\Delta(p) = J^*(\mathcal P) - J^*(\mathcal P \cup \{p\})$ until $N$ paths are selected. The per-generation cost is $O(N N_p T G + N_p^2 T)$, so running time grows roughly linearly with the fleet size.

## Conclusion

EMPSO combines motion encoding, path-space niching, a hybrid PSO/DE update, and fleet-aware extraction to plan spatially distinct trajectories for a UAV fleet in a single run. It achieves the best or highly competitive minimum search time on six benchmark scenarios at reasonable computational cost, and its paths execute as coordinated missions on real UAVs. Future work will parallelize the implementation for larger maps and fleets and relax the assumption of deterministic target motion.
